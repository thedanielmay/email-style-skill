# Voice: what the email says and how it sounds

Decided Thu 1 Oct 2026 over four content rounds. Built from about 130 of Daniel's own sent emails, Oct 2024 to Sep 2025. Newer mail was not used, because much of it is AI-written. Options marked "new" had no examples in his mail and were chosen as proposals.

`SKILL.md` covers layout. This file covers words. Use both.

## Step zero: ask for missing inputs (set Thu 1 Oct 2026)

Daniel's instruction: when his instructions leave gaps in the input these structures need, prompt him for it, as much as possible.

1. Name the purpose, then check the instruction against that purpose's row in the table below.
2. For every missing input, **ask Daniel before drafting**. Use one batched question (AskUserQuestion, or a short list), not a drip.
3. **Never fill a gap with a plausible guess.** A made-up date, figure, memory or reason reads exactly like a real one.
4. If he says "you decide" for an input, choose it, then mark the choice in the review notes.
5. Inputs that a record answers (an email thread, the Snapshot data, a calendar) are looked up rather than asked. Cite the source in the review notes.

| Purpose | Inputs needed |
|---|---|
| Any Full or Client email | Recipient and tier. The context line (what this follows on from). The ask, with **By**, **This unblocks** and **Reply with**. |
| Report or test results | What was checked, the result of each, and how sure: Tested, Read but not run, or From memory |
| Decision request | Each option and what it changes. What each decision holds up. A needed-by date. Daniel's suggestion, if he has one. Whether a default-on-silence is allowed. |
| Saying no | The reason (one line). The counter-offer: what he *can* do. |
| Bad news, his fault | What happened. Impact. What is already done. What happens next, and when. |
| Bad news, not his fault | The news. What stays on track. The next date. |
| Pushing back | His position. The reason or stake. The alternative he proposes. |
| Complaint | A dated timeline of facts. The ask. The boundary. Which step this is (first, or escalation). |
| Chase | What was promised and when. Which ladder step (1, 2 or 3). The date for step 2. The consequence for step 3. |
| Escalation | Who it goes to. The full dated history. The decision or action needed. |
| Thanks | What they did. What it made possible. |
| Praise | What worked. How he knows (the evidence). |
| Introduction | A two-line bio of each person. Why they should talk. The first step, and who takes it. Whether to ask each person first. |
| Condolence | The person's name and relationship. One real memory, if he has one. A concrete offer he will actually make. |
| Proposal | His recommendation. Why. Cost. The alternative. The decision date. |
| Rate or money ask | Hours or units, amount and currency, what it covers, the billing cycle |
| Time ask | How long. What he will send beforehand. Two specific times. |
| First contact | Who he is. How he found them. The need. The ask. |
| Grant or donation | The amount. What it pays for. Evidence as a number, from a real source. |
| Persuading a group | The recommendation. Why. The cost of doing nothing. The options. What he offers to do himself. |

## Habits to keep (from his 2024-25 mail)

- The ask on its own line, usually bold.
- A colon where others use a dash: "On the road: flaky internet."
- Apologise in one sentence, then move on.
- Offer the easy option next to the ask: "Happy to pick it up from the front desk if that is easier."
- A dated bullet timeline for any history (medical, billing, legal).
- Introductions: a two-line bio of each person, then the reason.
- In group threads, one line per person with their action: "Nick: ... Adam: ..."

## Habits to drop

- "Just" as a softener ("Just checking"). Write "Checking".
- "As per" openers.
- A warm sign-off after a complaint.
- Emoji to strangers or officials.
- Several separate asks. The ask box groups them.
- Unchecked typos. Every draft gets read through before preview.

## Sign-off by situation

| When | Sign-off |
|---|---|
| Routine, or the reader is doing something for him | Thanks,<br>Daniel |
| They went out of their way or did him a favour | Much thanks,<br>Daniel |
| A complaint, a boundary, or an escalation | Daniel (name only) |
| Nothing asked: a warm note, a check-in, a goodbye | All best,<br>Daniel |
| Condolence | Daniel |

Contact shortcuts in CLAUDE.md override all of these (Leemo: "Hey buddy," and "Pappa").

## Default tone by relationship

| Relationship | Default tone | Usual sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| Work peers and partners (Simon, Cade) | Warm and candid. Direct about money and problems. Bullets for anything dense. A ":)" is fine. | Thanks, or Much thanks, |
| Clients and funders | Warm, plain, a little more formal. No code, no emoji, no jokes. Always a next step with a date. | Thanks, |
| Boards and senior people | The most formal work voice. Recommendation first, evidence second. Short. | Thanks, |
| Church | Collegial and easy. Faith language only if Daniel writes it himself. Adam Hince emails also keep heavy nesting and flag departures from what he asked (`feedback-adam-email-format.md`). | Thanks, |
| Suppliers, clinics, services | Polite and very direct: the ask on its own line, the easy option next to it. Firm in complaints. | Thanks, Much thanks, or name only |
| Family and friends | Warm and plain, one fact per line. | Thanks, or All best, |
| Strangers | Who he is and how he found them first, then the ask. No emoji. | Thanks, |

**Emoji:** only with people he knows well. Never to strangers, officials or funders.

## Openings

- **Full and Client tiers:** the greeting, then one context line, then the ask box.
- **The context line carries any warmth.** Use something specific when there is something ("Hope the market day went well on Saturday"). Otherwise leave it out. Never write the generic "Hope you're well".

## Reporting

- **Result first, in bold.** Detail after. One line only when the reader already has the detail.
- **Label how sure each claim is:**
  - **Tested:** for what was run
  - **Read in the code, not run:** for what was only read
  - **From memory:** for anything recalled. This is rule 3, hedge the recall.
- **No soft hedges.** "Seems to", "should be" and "appears to" are banned when the claim can carry a label instead.

## Asks

- **The direct ask, then the easy option.**
  - ✅ "**Can you send it by Fri 7 Mar?** Happy to pick it up from the front desk if that is easier."
- **The ask, then one line of why.** The reason is what lets them say yes quickly.
- **Asking a busy person for time:** state the exact cost to them, what you will send beforehand, and two specific times.
- **First contact:** how you found them first, then the need, then the ask with the easy option.

## Saying no

- **The no, a one-line reason, then a counter-offer.** End on what he *can* do (rule 2).
  - ✅ "I can't run a second day at no charge: the first day was the pro bono part. **I can run a half-day on Thu 15 Oct at my usual rate, or send the notes free.** Which suits?"
- **Church and friends:** a warm no with a smaller yes ("I can train whoever takes it on").
  - A deferral ("ask me again in February") only when he might genuinely say yes later.

## Bad news

- **His fault:** what happened, then a one-sentence apology in the first two lines, then **Impact / Done / Next**.
- **Not his fault:** the news, what stays on track, then the next date. No apology, and no blame by name. Writing "sorry" for someone else's delay takes the blame.
- **"Unfortunately" never opens bad news.** State it plainly.

## Pushing back

- **His position in bold, the reason or stake, then a question offering another way.**
  - ✅ "**I'd rather not give volunteers the admin role.** It lets them see the medical fields. Could a read-only role with the three fields they need do the same job?"

## Complaints, chasing, escalation

- **Complaint:**
  - First time: dated facts as bullets, the ask in bold, then the boundary. Name-only sign-off.
  - Second time: the escalation version, with a date and the consequence.
- **Chasing ladder,** about 5 working days between steps:
  1. Friendly, with the easy option.
  2. Restate, with a date and the reason it matters.
  3. Escalate, with the consequence.
- **Escalation after step 3, by relationship.** Every step is drafted for Daniel's approval and never sent on a timer.

  | Relationship | Step 4 | Step 5 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Supplier, clinic, tradie | A call to the manager or owner, then an email confirming what was agreed | A formal complaint: Consumer Affairs Victoria, the relevant ombudsman, or a card chargeback |
  | Client or funder | A call, then an email summarising it | Their manager or board contact, on Cc, with a short factual email |
  | Work peer or partner | A 15-minute meeting, not email | Whoever owns the work on their side, on Cc, with the facts and the decision needed |
  | Church, friends, family | In person or a call | No written escalation |

  Every escalation email restates the whole history as a dated timeline, so the new reader needs nothing else.
- **Chasing a peer for a decision:** restate the ask in full and add a default: "If I don't hear by Fri 9 Oct, I'll go with my suggestion."
  - **Never use a default for money, people, or anything that can't be undone.**

## Relationship emails

- **Thanks:** what they did, then what it made possible.
- **Praise:** specific. Say what worked and how he knows it, with a number where there is one.
- **Introductions:**
  - **Default:** a two-line bio each, the reason, the first step for each person, then "I'll leave you both to it from here."
  - **Senior people or strangers:** ask each person first, separately (a double opt-in).
- **Condolence** (new, prose, exempt from bullets):
  - Name the person, never "your loss".
  - One real memory, if Daniel has one.
  - One concrete offer, never "let me know if there is anything".
  - "You don't need to reply."
- **Check-ins:** one sincere line.
- **One-line replies** ("Signed. Copy attached.") are allowed when the fragment is the whole reply, in the Light and Plain tiers. This is different from fragments used for rhythm, which CLAUDE.md bans.

## Persuasion

- **Proposal:**
  - **Default:** the recommendation first in bold, then **Why / Cost / Alternative**, then a dated ask.
  - **Three or more real options:** an options table with the suggestion marked.
- **Rate or money:** hours or units, the amount, and what it covers, stated as settled. No "Does this sound unreasonable?"
- **Grant or donation** (new): the amount, what it pays for, and the evidence as a number from a real source. Grant application answers themselves stay prose.
- **Persuading a sceptical group:**
  - the recommendation
  - why
  - **what doing nothing costs**
  - the options
  - what he offers to do himself

## Words to avoid

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Just" as a softener | Drop it: "Checking" |
| "As per" | "As you said", "Following" |
| "I hope this email finds you well", "Hope you're well" | Something specific, or nothing |
| "Reach out", "circle back", "touch base" | "Contact", "come back to", "talk" |
| "Utilise", "leverage", "facilitate" | "Use", "help", "arrange" |
| "Kind regards", "Best regards" | The sign-offs above |
| "Please don't hesitate to contact me" | Nothing. It is a closer, and closers are banned. |
| "Unfortunately" at the start of bad news | State the news plainly |
| "ASAP" | A real date |
| "Seems to", "should be", "appears to" in reports | A certainty label |

## Telegraphic wording

Only on request ("telegraphic"). Cut words, never facts. The rules and guardrails are in `SKILL.md`, section "Telegraphic style (only when Daniel asks)". It suits a reader who shares the context, such as Adam with the Doc open. It reads curt to strangers, funders and officials, so ask first.
